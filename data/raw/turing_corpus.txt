Alan Turing was born in London in 1912.
Alan Turing studied at King's College, Cambridge.
Alan Turing later studied at Princeton University under Alonzo Church.
In 1936, Alan Turing published "On Computable Numbers", which introduced the idea of the Turing machine.
During World War II, Alan Turing worked at Bletchley Park for the Government Code and Cypher School.
Alan Turing helped design the Bombe to break Enigma-encrypted messages.
After the war, Alan Turing worked at the National Physical Laboratory and proposed the Automatic Computing Engine.
Alan Turing later joined the University of Manchester and contributed to the Manchester Mark I.
In 1950, Alan Turing proposed the Turing Test in the paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence".
Alan Turing died in Wilmslow, England, in 1954.
